The Many Portraits of Juan José Cabezudo

How many portraits can one person have? A visual history of the Afro-Peruvian figure Juan José Cabezudo examines nineteenth-century portrayals, contemporary LGBTQ+ memory work and representations produced by AI chatbots.

Laura Guien, Roland Alvarez Chavez, Elena Hernandez CameroIllustrated digital carouselWho Owns Memory? · 2026

Encounter the story

History through places, objects and voices

Juan José Cabezudo (c. 1800–1860), an Afro-Peruvian cook in early republican Lima, is widely considered Peru's first openly queer public figure. This ten-slide Instagram carousel follows the successive portraits of him from the watercolors of Francisco Javier Cortés and Pancho Fierro, Courret's studio photograph, to the contemporary memory work of Estampas Maricas de Lima. It points out the known biases of each of these representations, then examines a fifth portrait: the one AI chatbots produce today.

The chapters below bring the contributor’s sources and images into a readable, illustrated public-history narrative. Photographs and visual records can be enlarged by selecting them.
