About the project

Juan José Cabezudo (c. 1800–1860), an Afro-Peruvian cook in early republican Lima, is widely considered Peru's first openly queer public figure. This ten-slide Instagram carousel follows the successive portraits of him from the watercolors of Francisco Javier Cortés and Pancho Fierro, Courret's studio photograph, to the contemporary memory work of Estampas Maricas de Lima. It points out the known biases of each of these representations, then examines a fifth portrait: the one AI chatbots produce today.

Historical setting

Lima, Peru, from the 1820s to the 1860s (early republican period), and today's digital space. The story concerns Juan José Cabezudo and, through him, Afro-Peruvian and LGBTQ+ histories in Lima, as well as the contemporary Peruvian memory work that reclaims them. It is transnational in its production (Peru / Europe) and primarily digital in its form and in its object: how AI systems now mediate access to marginalized pasts.

Why this story matters

For many young people, a chatbot is now the first point of contact with the past. Lives like Cabezudo's (Black, queer and working-class) entered the record only through other people's eyes, and each of those portraits carried its maker's bias. Historical images can still be questioned: they have an author, a date, a caption. A chatbot's answer has none, and it can change from one question to the next. By moving from historical visual representations to contemporary algorithmic representation, the project raises a warning without demonizing the technology: chatbots, like the painters before them, carry Cabezudo's memory forward, but never neutrally. The goal is to give young audiences, especially young LGBTQ+ people, the tools to ask who made the portrait they are looking at, including when it comes from a chatbot.

The guiding question

What survives of marginalized memories like Juan José Cabezudo's when young people ask an AI directly, without the sources and institutions that once contextualized his image? And what should they know before they trust the answer?
