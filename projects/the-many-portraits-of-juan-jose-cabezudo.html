The Many Portraits of Juan José Cabezudo

How many portraits can one person have? A visual history of the Afro-Peruvian figure Juan José Cabezudo examines nineteenth-century portrayals, contemporary LGBTQ+ memory work and representations produced by AI chatbots.

Digital Memory & DemocracyLaura Guien, Roland Alvarez Chavez, Elena Hernandez CameroIllustrated digital carousel

The historical story

Juan José Cabezudo was a well-known Afro-Peruvian cook in nineteenth-century Lima, represented in watercolours, studio photography and later cultural memory. These images are historical sources shaped by the viewpoints of their makers.

The ten-slide project moves from nineteenth-century depictions by Francisco Javier Cortés and Pancho Fierro to contemporary reinterpretation and the unpredictable “portraits” now offered by AI chatbots.

It invites audiences to read portraits critically: who made the image, what sources supported it, and whose historical voice remains absent?

03

Places, memory and interpretation

Lima, Peru, from the 1820s to the present, with particular attention to the representation of Afro-Peruvian and LGBTQ+ histories.

Public-history question

What changes when an AI system becomes the first portrait of a historical figure?

Sources, evidence and credits
