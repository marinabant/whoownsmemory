Testimony & Family Memory

Rebuilding Memory at the Hatay Assembly Building

Yusuf Düzel•Photo essay•Solo

The story

This photo essay follows the Hatay Assembly Building, locally known as the Meclis, through eight photographs I took between November 2022 and April 2026. It brings together historical sources and my memories of it as a social space, my first return after earthquake displacement and seeing the building rebuilt. Through these changes, I explore how reconstruction can reconnect a familiar place with personal and public memory.

Where and when?

The story is situated in central Antakya (historically known as Antiochia), Hatay, Turkey around the former Hatay State assembly building at Köprübaşı, beside the river and Atatürk Caddesi. My photographs cover November 2022 to April 2026 before and after the February 2023 earthquakes and during reconstruction. Historical context includes the building’s assembly use in 1938 and its later cultural role. Antakya is a multicultural city whose residents have different linguistic, religious and cultural backgrounds. The essay explores a familiar landmark within this shared urban setting through my own memories and photographs. I was displaced to Ankara after the earthquake and moved back to Hatay in September 2024. My experience offers one perspective on loss, reconstruction and belonging, without claiming to represent every community in the region. The project is a local, place based story presented as a digital photo essay

Central public-history question

When a historic building returns, how much of the place we remember returns with it?

Why does this story matter?

The Meclis connects Hatay’s political history with everyday life in Antakya. For me, it was a meeting point in the city and a place that represents the multicultural face of the city. Seeing it damaged after the earthquake made the city’s losses feel concentrated in one familiar building. Seeing it rebuilt brought hope and a sense of home. My photographs preserve the damaged and unfinished stages alongside its renewed appearance, inviting readers to consider how buildings, personal memories and the spaces around them contribute to a city’s history.

Sources & evidence

Primary material consists of eight photographs I took between 29 November 2022 and 26 April 2026, accompanied by my personal recollections.
