The story

What can the journey of three Argentine born German siblings to Germany and back tell us about Germanness abroad and the Second War World beyond German borders? What happens when an imagined homeland becomes a place you need to leave? Following this family from Argentina to Germany and back complicates the familiar postwar story of the Second War World and Nazis fleeing to Argentina. Their journey reveals earlier transatlantic movements through which ideas of Germanness, family ties, education and political affiliations circulated between Germany and Argentina

Where and when?

An argentina-german family that migrates from Argentina to Germay and back between 1938-1948

Central public-history question

What new insights about germaness and the Second World War can be obtain following the trip of an argentinean-german family?

Why does this story matter?

Connections between Argentina and national socialism existed well before the end of the war when former nazis arrived at the country as “refugees”. This family story shows that there existed movements in the opposite direction because of the transnational circulation of families, languages, values, etc.
