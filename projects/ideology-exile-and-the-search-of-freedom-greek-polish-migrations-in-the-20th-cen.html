The story

Inspired by our own family histories, this project explores two interconnected migration experiences: Greek political refugees who settled in Poland after the Greek Civil War and Polish emigrants who left the country during the communist period. By bringing these stories together, we aim to raise public awareness of migration histories that remain largely overlooked and to highlight the shared experiences, connections, and memories that link Greece and Poland. Through a digital exhibition based on a timeline, we invite audiences to reflect on migration, displacement and belonging in Cold War Europe.

Where and when?

The story is set in Greece and Poland between the end of the Second World War and 1991. It explores the interconnected migration histories of Greek political refugees and Polish emigrants, highlighting the experiences of both communities through a transnational digital narrative.

Central public-history question

How did the Cold War shape migration experiences in Greece and Poland, and what connections can we draw between them?

Why does this story matter?

It explores a lesser-known and rarely discussed chapter of European migration history. The project aims to highlight the historical connections between the two countries and shed light on the different political and personal circumstances that shaped people’s decisions to migrate. It also encourages audiences to reflect on migration, displacement and memory.
