The project · Salamis, Greece

Living with an ancient past

A story about the island of Salamis and how its people relate to its classical past.

Antonis Chaliakopoulos’s digital exhibition asks how the people of Salamis relate to the classical past and, specifically, the memory of the Battle of Salamis. The project is situated on the contemporary island, using photographs taken between 2024 and 2026 from the creator’s personal archive.

The original digital work is available on the contributor’s chosen interactive platform. This accompanying exhibition page introduces the author’s question and its historical context without replacing the interactive work or reproducing photographs that were not provided separately for this edition.

01

An island and its remembered past

Contemporary Salamis · 2024–2026

Salamis is an inhabited island with a history that reaches far beyond the naval battle of 480 BCE. In the contributor’s framing, the focus is not only on the ancient event, but on the people who live in its landscape today.

The project asks what the classical past means in the present: how an internationally recognised historical episode is encountered, remembered and interpreted in everyday life. The personal photographic archive, made between 2024 and 2026, provides the visual basis for the creator’s original interactive story.

02

Why Darius’ ghost?

Aeschylus · The Persians

The project’s title evokes a figure from Aeschylus’ tragedy The Persians, first performed in 472 BCE, eight years after the naval battle of Salamis. The play recounts the Persian defeat, from the perspective of the Persian court, where the ghost of the former king Darius is summoned.

This ancient framing introduces a productive question for public history: a celebrated event can be transmitted through more than one voice, and each retelling has its own perspective. The Battle of Salamis survives not simply as a date or a location, but through interpretations that change as audiences and contexts change.

Additional curatorial context

The reference to Aeschylus is provided here to explain the title. It draws on The Persians and is separate from the contributor’s unspecified project bibliography. It is not presented as a quotation from the author’s digital exhibition.

A source across centuries

The Persians

In the tragedy, the defeated Persian side speaks; Darius’ ghost returns to hear about the disastrous campaign. Reading the ancient work can open a discussion about the perspective from which a celebrated battle is remembered.
