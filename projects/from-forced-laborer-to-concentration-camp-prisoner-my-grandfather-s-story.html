Testimony & Family Memory

From Forced Laborer to Concentration Camp Prisoner: My Grandfather's Story

Daria Vystavkina•Short video•Solo

The story

This short film reconstructs the fragments of my grandfather Georgiy Vystavkin’s life: a Ukrainian taken to Germany as a forced laborer and held in four Nazi camps. Through archival records, newspaper fragments, historical traces, and family memories, the film searches for the person behind the evidence

Where and when?

The story is situated between Ukraine and Nazi Germany, across the twentieth century, with its core in 1943 to 1945.It concerns Ukrainian forced labourers, the Ostarbeiter, roughly two million Ukrainians among the millions deported to work for German industry, a group whose story is still little known. At its centre is one individual, my grandfather, and one family across three generations. Central public-history question

Why do some histories stay untold, and what public responsibility do we carry for them? The film approaches this through the forced labourers deported to Germany, whose stories were silenced by shame and marginalization, both in their own societies and in how the Second World War is publicly remembered.

Why does this story matter?

Because it belongs to millions and is almost never told. The two million Ukrainians deported to Germany as forced labourers were silenced by shame and suspicion, and their memory was lost before it could become public. Recovering one life shows what that silence cost, and why, in a time of renewed war and deportation, these stories still matter.

Sources & evidence

Archival records. Prisoner records and the camp-number index obtained on request from the Arolsen Archives (Bad Arolsen), the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, and the Dachau Concentration Camp Memorial. These establish the dates, camps, and prisoner numbers of my grandfather's path through the camp system. Family archive. Photographs, identity documents on the restoration of his name, and a video recording of my grandfather with his great-grandson. Press sources. Two Heilbronn newspaper reports on the Kochendorf camp: Angela Groß in Region Heilbronn (2008) and Sandra Weckert in Heilbronner Stimme (2008). Scholarship. On the Nazi forced-labor system and its scale: Ulrich Herbert, Fremdarbeiter (1985); Mark Spoerer, Zwangsarbeit unter dem Hakenkreuz (2001); Spoerer and Fleischhacker, "Forced Laborers in Nazi Germany" (2002). On the Kochendorf salt-mine camp: Klaus Riexinger and Detlef Ernst, Vernichtung durch Arbeit (2003). On Ukrainian forced laborers: Tetiana Pastushenko (Ukrainian Jewish Encounter).

Public-history lens

This project is presented within Testimony, Family Memory & Community Archives. Read it not only for what happened in the past, but for how sources, memory, place, visibility and interpretation shape what becomes public history.
