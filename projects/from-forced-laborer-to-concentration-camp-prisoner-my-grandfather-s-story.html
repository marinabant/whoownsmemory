Content note

This exhibition concerns Nazi persecution, forced labour and concentration-camp imprisonment. It presents family history without reproducing graphic imagery.

01 / The question

The life behind the evidence

Why do some histories stay untold, and what public responsibility do we carry for them?

“This short film reconstructs the fragments of my grandfather Georgiy Vystavkin’s life: a Ukrainian taken to Germany as a forced laborer and held in four Nazi camps. Through archival records, newspaper fragments, historical traces, and family memories, the film searches for the person behind the evidence.”

Daria Vystavkina’s short film reconstructs fragments of her grandfather Georgiy Vystavkin’s life. A Ukrainian deported to Germany as a forced labourer, he was subsequently held in four Nazi camps. Archival documents, press reports and family memories are brought together to look beyond the administrative record and towards the person who lived through these experiences.

The film moves between individual memory and a wider history of forced labour, imprisonment and post-war silence. Its central years are 1943–1945, with family memory connecting the events to later generations.

03 / Research & testimony

How the story was reconstructed

The film follows one family across three generations and a historical geography spanning Ukraine and Nazi Germany, especially the years 1943–1945. It draws on institutional records, family materials, press reporting, and scholarly research. Read together, these sources help reconstruct a life without suggesting that the archival record is complete.

Institutional records

Prisoner records and camp-number information were obtained on request from the Arolsen Archives, Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, and Dachau Concentration Camp Memorial. The contributor identifies these records as the basis for reconstructing dates, camps and prisoner numbers.

Family archive

Family photographs, identity documents concerning the restoration of Georgiy Vystavkin’s name, and a family video preserve aspects of his life that institutional files cannot convey.

Press and scholarship

Two 2008 Heilbronn newspaper reports discuss Kochendorf. Research by Ulrich Herbert, Mark Spoerer and others situates this life within the Nazi forced-labour system.

04 / Memory & responsibility

A silence carried across generations

The project places the experiences of Ukrainian Ostarbeiter in view: civilians deported for forced labour in Nazi Germany. In her final submission, Vystavkina describes roughly two million Ukrainians among those deported for this work. She emphasises the shame, suspicion and marginalisation that contributed to survivors’ experiences remaining outside public memory.

Recovering one life reveals what prolonged silence can mean for a family. The film also asks why these histories matter amid renewed experiences of war and deportation, and how descendants can recount persecution responsibly when much of the surviving evidence was generated by institutions of violence.

Curatorial note

The contributor’s final submission establishes the principal biographical outline and identifies the relevant archives. The documents themselves and the film’s detailed camp-by-camp chronology have not been independently re-examined for this page. No dates, prisoner numbers, testimonies or quotations have been added beyond the submitted account.
