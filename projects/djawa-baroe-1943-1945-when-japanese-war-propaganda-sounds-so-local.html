The story

Trend progression recorded on Japanese wartime propagandic magazine Djawa Baroe (1943-1945)

Where and when?

Dutch East Indies (modern-day Indonesia)

Central public-history question

How did the Japanese Empire attempt to sway Indonesian public opinion via mass media during the Second World War?

Why does this story matter?

It uncovers how aesthetic takes an important part in wartime propaganda, and in the case of the Japanese Occupation, the localisation of the aesthetic and messaging was utilised to sway public opinion. In Indonesian collective memory, the narrative revolving the initial involvement of Indonesian people in support of the Japanese Empire has not been discussed as often as the nationalist narrative. However, this project highlights the complexity of war in displaying the ambiguity of victim and perpetrator narrative to a certain extent, that is, until the façade could no longer be sustained by mass media propaganda.
