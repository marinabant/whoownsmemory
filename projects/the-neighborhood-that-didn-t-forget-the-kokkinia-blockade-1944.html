The story

This project examines the 1944 Kokkinia Blockade and traces how the local community preserved and contested its memory through monuments, film, plaques, and commemorative practices from 1956 to the present. It asks “who owns memory?” by showing how a working-class neighbourhood kept alive an anti-fascist narrative despite persecution, censorship, and distortion.

Where and when?

This project is situated in Kokkinia/Nikaia, a suburb of Piraeus, during the German Occupation of the Second World War, focusing on the blockade and mass executions of 17 August 1944. It concerns the local working-class community of Kokkinia, which was targeted because of its active participation in the Resistance. The story extends into the post-war decades through commemorate acts, showing how local memory interacted with broader European debates about fascism and remembrance.

Central public-history question

Who owns the memory of the Kokkinia Blockade, and how has a local community sustained that memory in public space?

Why does this story matter?

This story matters because the public needs to know its own history, particularly traumatic episodes such as Nazi war crimes and collective punishments like the Kokkinia Blockade. Remembering these events raises awareness about the mechanisms of fascism and occupation violence and, above all, helps ensure that societies recognize and resist the revival of such ideologies
