Migration & Belonging

The Ghana Must Go Bag: When an Object Outlives Its Memory

Doris Anderson•Digital exhibition / web story•Solo

The story

A short web story about the checked woven plastic bag known across West Africa as "Ghana Must Go". The name comes from Nigeria's expulsion of Ghanaian and other West African migrants in January 1983. The story follows the bag from an ordinary carrier, to a name that records an expulsion, to a pattern now remade by artists and fashion designers. It asks what happens when an object stays familiar while the history behind its name fades. It uses historical scholarship, published testimony, two openly licensed photographs, an interactive timeline and a route map. Live version: https://ghana-must-go-story.netlify.app

Where and when?

The story is set in Nigeria and Ghana, along the coastal road from Lagos through Benin and Togo to Accra. Its central moment is January 1983, within a longer period from Ghana's Aliens Compliance Order of 1969 to Nigeria's second expulsion in 1985, and it follows the bag's later life up to the present. It concerns Ghanaian migrants who lived and worked in Nigeria and were ordered to leave, their memory of that return, and the wider West African public that still uses the bag and its name. The project is transnational.

Central public-history question

How does an everyday object become a carrier of collective memory, and what happens when the object remains recognizable while the history that gave it meaning begins to fade?

Why does this story matter?

Millions of people use this bag and say its name without knowing that the name records a mass expulsion. That makes it a clear case of how memory can survive in everyday language and objects while the knowledge behind it thins out. The story also speaks to the present: migrants are still blamed for economic trouble, and free movement in West Africa is still contested. And as the pattern moves onto runways and into galleries, it raises the question the programme asks: who owns this memory, and who gets to remake it?
