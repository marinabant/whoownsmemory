The Ghana Must Go Bag: When an Object Outlives Its Memory

A familiar woven bag carries the memory of Nigeria’s 1983 expulsion of Ghanaian migrants. This object history traces how an everyday item acquired a name tied to displacement and then travelled into fashion and global culture.

Encounter the story

History through places, objects and voices

A short web story about the checked woven plastic bag known across West Africa as "Ghana Must Go". The name comes from Nigeria's expulsion of Ghanaian and other West African migrants in January 1983. The story follows the bag from an ordinary carrier, to a name that records an expulsion, to a pattern now remade by artists and fashion designers. It asks what happens when an object stays familiar while the history behind its name fades. It uses historical scholarship, published testimony, two openly licensed photographs, an interactive timeline and a route map.

The chapters below bring the contributor’s sources and images into a readable, illustrated public-history narrative. Photographs and visual records can be enlarged by selecting them.

The bag as transnational memory

Chapter 01

You know the bag. Do you know the story?

The checked woven-plastic carrier bag is familiar in markets, transport hubs and households across West Africa. Yet the words “Ghana Must Go” register a historical experience that many contemporary users no longer associate with the object.

Chapter 02

A name born of expulsion

The bag existed before the expression. Its name became associated with Nigeria’s expulsion of Ghanaians and other foreign residents in 1983. A commonplace object thus carries an order directed at people who were forced to leave.

Chapter 03

1983: when “go” became a command

On 17 January 1983, Nigerian authorities announced that unskilled migrants should leave by the end of the month. Economic crisis and political scapegoating shaped the expulsion. Estimates of those displaced vary substantially; researchers caution against repeating a single figure without qualification.

Chapter 04

What can a bag carry?

The journey towards Ghana often took people through Benin and Togo. Migrants carried clothes and belongings in easily available bags, but the objects could not preserve the homes, relationships and livelihoods they had been compelled to abandon.

Chapter 05

An object becomes an archive

The expression outlived the political event. In subsequent decades, a bag used for ordinary travel and trade also preserved the memory of a state-led expulsion, even when that connection was no longer widely understood.

Chapter 06

Remade and reimagined

The checked bag has been remade in fashion, art and everyday commerce. Such reinterpretations can increase its visibility while obscuring the history of displacement behind its name.

Chapter 07

When a name outlives its memory

The project distinguishes documented historical events from later popular interpretations of the bag. It asks what happens when words endure but the histories that produced them fade.

Chapter 08

What the bag remembers

The story returns the bag to a history of movement, exclusion and belonging. A familiar object can open a public conversation about borders, political responsibility and the everyday material traces of forced migration.
