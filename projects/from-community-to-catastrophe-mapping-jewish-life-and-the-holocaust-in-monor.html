From Community to Catastrophe

Mapping Jewish Life and the Holocaust in Monor

The public-history question
Where were local Jewish institutions and central locations situated, how were they embedded in the town’s spatial structure?
Why this story matters

The local survivors collected sources and recollections about the community’s past, and published their „Yizkor” book privately, which is not available to the public. In 2024, a local teacher and I compiled the short history of the community which was also published, but reached a limited audience. As Monor was the site of an active community and a major collecting camp in 1944, I believe it is essential to show the traces of Jewish life and Holocaust-related locations to the locals.

CHAPTER 01

Before the catastrophe

The Jewish community of Monor was established in 1862 by twenty-five families. A synagogue, shops, homes and places of work became part of the town’s everyday landscape. The map makes this urban history visible, inviting visitors to begin with lives and institutions rather than only with their destruction.

CHAPTER 02

A geography transformed by persecution

In 1944, anti-Jewish measures transformed familiar streets into a geography of surveillance, confinement and deportation. The StoryMap connects the sites of Jewish communal life with the spaces through which its residents were displaced and persecuted.

CHAPTER 03

Reading absence in the present

A map can show what is no longer visible. By placing photographs, addresses and documents onto a contemporary street plan, this project asks how a town can recognise both its Jewish heritage and the violence through which that heritage was ruptured.
