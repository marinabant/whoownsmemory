About the project

In 1862, 25 families established the Jewish community of Monor. This prosperous congregation had their own synagogue and institutions, while the local Jews played an active role in the town’s economic and social life. However, the anti-Jewish persecution put an abrupt and tragic end to the coexistence of Jews and non-Jews: the Jews were forced to move into designated houses, then they were taken to the local brick factory which served as a collection site for 9,000 Jews from Monor and the surrounding areas. The victims were deported to Auschwitz-Birkenau in three trains on 6 and 8 July 1944. This project will show the most important locations central to Jewish life in Monor before and during the Holocaust.

Historical setting

Monor, the history of the Jewish community between 1862 and 1944

Why this story matters

The local survivors collected sources and recollections about the community’s past, and published their „Yizkor” book privately, which is not available to the public. In 2024, a local teacher and I compiled the short history of the community which was also published, but reached a limited audience. As Monor was the site of an active community and a major collecting camp in 1944, I believe it is essential to show the traces of Jewish life and Holocaust-related locations to the locals.

The guiding question

Where were local Jewish institutions and central locations situated, how were they embedded in the town’s spatial structure?
