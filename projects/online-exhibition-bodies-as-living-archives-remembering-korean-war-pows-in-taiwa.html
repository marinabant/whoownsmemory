Bodies as Living Archives: Remembering Korean War POWs in Taiwan

The experiences of Chinese Korean War prisoners of war who came to Taiwan in 1954 were shaped by official Cold War narratives. This digital exhibition places personal memory, the body and artistic research alongside state archives.

The historical story

In 1954, more than 14,000 Chinese prisoners of war from the Korean War arrived in Taiwan. Official representation cast them as “Anti-Communist Heroes”, while their own experiences of war, displacement and settlement were far more varied.

Hsiao-Chi Chu’s digital exhibition draws on socially engaged artistic work, photographs, testimony and encounters with former POWs at the Taipei Veterans Home between 2014 and 2018.

The body becomes a living archive: gestures, silences and everyday recollections complicate the categories imposed by political institutions.

Places, memory and interpretation

Archival photographs and historical documents from Taiwanese public collections, including materials related to the arrival of Korean War POWs and the construction of the “Anti-Communist Hero” narrative. 5. Selected historical and scholarly research on Korean War POWs, their resettlement in Taiwan and Cold War memory.
