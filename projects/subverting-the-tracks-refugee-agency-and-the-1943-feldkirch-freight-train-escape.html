War & Difficult Pasts

Subverting the Tracks: Refugee Agency and the 1943 Feldkirch Freight Train Escape

Marie Jensen•Digital exhibition / web story•Solo

The story

The forgotten history of the 1943 Feldkirch Freight Train Escape reveals the current German- centric gap in Holocaust education. Through this example, a narrative of active subversion in intense surveillance networks is discovered. By reflecting on the public invisibility of this physical resistance in a relatively unknown city in mainstream Holocaust education, this calls for the essential digital public intervention that shatters the passive-victim stereotype and allows for the Holocaust to be taught as a globalized event.

Where and when?

My story is situated in Feldkirch, Austria in 1943. The story focuses on the group of refugees who attempted to escape into Switzerland using a freight train going through Feldkirch. My main audience for this story is Holocaust educators, museum curators, and students learning about the Holocaust.

Central public-history question

How does the forgotten history of the 1943 freight train escape redefine our understanding of active refugee resistance, and how does shifting our lens to this Austrian border node challenge the German-centric biases of mainstream Holocaust education?

Why does this story matter?

Mainstream Holocaust education today has conditioned the public to view the railway system as only vehicles of victimhood. I argue that by looking at the small city of Feldkirch and instances of resistance that happened here, Holocaust educators can show the public audience that the Holocaust is not as German-centric as they may have been otherwise taught as well as that these marginalized stories reveal moments of resistance.
