01 / The story

Who has the authority to tell Amo’s story?

Anton Wilhelm Amo’s life crosses the boundaries of conventional European intellectual history. His subsequent remembrance crosses further boundaries: between libraries, cities, monuments, community organisations and generations.

The Anton Wilhelm Amo Heritage Audio Archive brings historical research into conversation with oral histories, recordings, images, places and the work of people who have kept Amo’s memory visible. The archive presents multiple perspectives on how an eighteenth-century African philosopher is represented in public space today.

Its organising principle is listening. Historical evidence and community knowledge are encountered together, without treating any one institution as the sole authority over memory.

Central public-history question

“Who has the authority to map, preserve and narrate Anton Wilhelm Amo’s memory?”

02 / Places & histories

A memory that travels

The archive begins with Amo’s life between West Africa and eighteenth-century Europe. He studied at the University of Halle, completed a doctorate at Wittenberg and taught at German universities. Today, the traces of his life and changing public reception are distributed across many sites and collections.

The project connects places associated with his intellectual history and later remembrance to networks of community knowledge in Europe and West Africa. These places are not presented as one fixed itinerary: the audiowalk supports different routes and remote exploration.

03 / Methods & participation

What can a moving archive do?

The project combines digital mapping, sound, historical sources and community perspectives. It offers ways to discover where memory is made—and whose knowledge has been left out of more conventional accounts.

01 / SOUND

Oral histories

Recorded conversations and contemporary voices accompany archival records and historical interpretations.

02 / PLACE

Site-based listening

Locative audio connects streets, university sites, monuments and memorial landscapes with public-history questions.

03 / EVIDENCE

Research and archives

Historical documents, Amo’s surviving writings, photographs and scholarship support the project’s different accounts.

04 / COMMUNITY

Shared interpretation

Community initiatives, researchers, artists and activists contribute distinct forms of knowledge and memory work.

05 / ACCESS

Multiple entry points

German- and English-language materials can be explored at relevant sites or remotely, without a single prescribed route.

06 / RESPONSIBILITY

Unfinished histories

Gaps, differing interpretations and the politics of representation remain visible instead of being resolved into one final narrative.
