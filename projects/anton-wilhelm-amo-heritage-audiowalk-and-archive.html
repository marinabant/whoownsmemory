01 / Research question

Who has the authority to tell Amo’s story?

Anton Wilhelm Amo Heritage: Sites of Memory presents the Anton Wilhelm Amo Heritage Audio Archive as a translocal and community-led public-history project concerned with the contested memory of the eighteenth-century African philosopher Anton Wilhelm Amo.

Rather than reconstructing Amo through a single authoritative biography, the project connects places, voices, archival traces, images, audio recordings and contemporary memory practices across Germany. It brings academic research into conversation with community knowledge, local memory work, artistic practice and the perspectives of people and initiatives who have shaped how Amo is remembered today.

The archive can be understood as a moving archive: knowledge about Amo does not remain in one institution, collection or national history, but circulates between places, communities and generations. Like a kind of weather map of memory, the project makes visible where memories accumulate, where narratives collide, where silences remain and where new forms of remembrance emerge.

The exhibition contribution is adapted from my scholarly work “Mapping Amo, Hearing Place: The Anton Wilhelm Amo Heritage Audio Archive as Black Cartography and Locative Sound Art” (DOI: 10.11588/artdok.00010180).

Who has the authority to map, preserve and narrate Anton Wilhelm Amo’s memory – and what changes when archives, communities, places and institutions are allowed to speak alongside rather than through one another?
02 / Translocal memory

A memory that travels

Amo’s life and intellectual work connect West Africa and eighteenth-century Europe. The archive follows the shifting locations and public interpretations through which his memory has survived.

The project links sites of memory in Berlin, Halle, Wittenberg and Jena with knowledge networks in Ghana and the Netherlands. Each place prompts questions about whose evidence is heard and whose interpretations shape public remembrance.

03 / The approach

Listening as historical method

Anton Wilhelm Amo is increasingly visible in European public memory, yet the knowledge through which his life is reconstructed remains fragmented across countries, archives, institutions, scholarly traditions and community histories.

This matters because public history is never only about recovering information from the past. It is also about deciding which sources count, whose knowledge becomes visible, who is authorised to interpret historical absence, and where memory is allowed to reside.

The Anton Wilhelm Amo Heritage Audio Archive responds to this problem by treating place, sound and community knowledge as historical sources in their own right. Rather than presenting one final version of Amo’s biography, the archive makes disagreement and fragmentation visible. Different interpretations, absences and memory traditions can coexist.

Accessibility is therefore not only a technical question within the project. It is also epistemic and geographical: how can people gain access to knowledge that is dispersed between institutions, countries and communities? How can public history make visible voices and forms of knowledge that have often remained outside conventional archives?

The project is freely accessible, bilingual in German and English, multimodal and designed to be experienced both at physical sites of memory and remotely. Audio, text, images, maps, transcripts where available, captions and source material provide different routes into the archive.

In this sense, the project does not simply digitise an existing archive. It asks what an archive can become when memory is understood as mobile, contested and collectively produced.
