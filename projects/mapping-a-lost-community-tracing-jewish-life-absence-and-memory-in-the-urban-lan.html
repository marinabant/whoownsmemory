Place & Local Memory

Mapping a Lost Community: Tracing Jewish Life, Absence and Memory in the Urban Landscape of Ioannina

Christos Karzis•Digital exhibition / web story•Solo

The story

This digital public history project traces the historical presence of the Jewish community of Ioannina through selected places in the city. Focusing on Jewish neighbourhoods, religious and educational sites, the deportation of 1944, and the traces that remain in the present-day urban landscape, the project explores how Jewish presence, destruction, absence and memory can be read through place. An interactive map connects surviving, lost and transformed sites with the contemporary city.

Where and when?

The story is situated in Ioannina, northwestern Greece, and focuses on the city’s historic Romaniote Jewish community. It follows the community’s relationship with the urban landscape from its documented medieval presence through the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the rupture caused by the deportation of the Jewish population in March 1944, and the traces of Jewish life that remain in the present-day city.

Central public-history question

How can we read the traces of a community in a city where much of its physical presence has disappeared or changed?

Why does this story matter?

This story matters because much of the historic Jewish presence in Ioannina is no longer immediately visible in the contemporary city. By connecting surviving places with sites that have disappeared or been transformed, the project makes this history more accessible to present-day residents and visitors. It also shows how urban space can preserve traces of both everyday communal life and historical rupture, encouraging reflection on how absence itself can become part of public memory.

Sources & evidence

Schminck-Gustavus, Μνήμες Κατοχής: Ιταλοί και Γερμανοί στα Γιάννενα και η καταστροφή της εβραϊκής κοινότητας, vol. 2 (2008); Hagen Fleischer, Στέμμα και Σβάστικα: Η Ελλάδα της Κατοχής και της Αντίστασης 1941–1944, vol. 2 (1995); and the historical material of the Central Board of Jewish Communities in Greece on the Jewish Community of Ioannina.

Public-history lens

This project is presented within Place, Local Memory, Monuments & Heritage. Read it not only for what happened in the past, but for how sources, memory, place, visibility and interpretation shape what becomes public history.
