01 / Project question

What remained of being young under forced labour?

In 1943, at the age of fifteen, Mieczysław Gnojek was deported from Poland to Germany for forced labour. The project uses his later published recollections to examine youth, coercion and survival under National Socialism.

The contributor’s interactive audio story places a rotary telephone at its centre. Yet no telephone line led to the forced labourers: every apparent “call” is a mediated encounter with testimony recorded and read decades later. The project asks who gets to speak, and what the passage of time changes in the act of listening.

03 / Original interactive experience

04 / Historical context

A youth shaped by coercion

Nazi Germany relied on a large system of coerced civilian labour across occupied Europe. Young people were deported, registered and made to work under restrictions that governed their mobility, food and daily lives.

The contributor’s sources include a published account of Gnojek’s experience, a contemporary study of wartime diets and the Arolsen Archives guide to the Arbeitsbuch (labour book). Such evidence allows readers to distinguish memory, administration and historical context.

05 / Curatorial reflection

Who speaks for a remembered experience?

The project’s submitted audio format includes recorded readings and transcripts in English, German and Polish. The recordings themselves were not provided in a form that could be safely embedded in this standalone page; they are not recreated or synthesised here.

The team initially hoped to work across Polish and Greek experiences. Permission conditions for a Greek survivor’s testimony were clarified late in the process, and the team excluded that testimony rather than uploading its transcript to an AI service contrary to the archive’s terms. This version therefore centres on the published Polish memoir alone.
