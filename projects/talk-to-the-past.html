Talk to the Past

An interactive rotary telephone introduces the memories of Mieczysław Gnojek, deported from Poland to Germany for forced labour in 1943 at the age of fifteen. The interface asks what was lost when childhood and youth were interrupted.

The historical story

The rotary telephone is an intentionally paradoxical interface: no working line connects the present to a person deported for forced labour in Nazi Germany. Instead, a visitor “calls” a memory.

The project uses records of Mieczysław Gnojek, deported from Poland in 1943 at the age of fifteen, and contemporary readings of recollections to examine how forced labour disrupted youth.

Its interpretive frame encourages reflection on source mediation and the responsibilities involved in reading, performing and remembering another person’s testimony.
