About the project

An interactive audio story using a rotary-telephone interface to explore the memories of Mieczysław Gnojek, deported from Poland to Germany for forced labour in 1943 at the age of fifteen. The recordings and reflections examine youth, coercion, memory and the ethics of representing forced labour.

Historical setting

The project focuses on the experiences and memories of young people subjected to forced labour in Nazi Germany. Its initial transnational scope considered both Polish and Greek experiences; the interactive story centres on the testimony of Mieczysław Gnojek.

Why this story matters

The experiences of forced labourers are central to the history of the Second World War, yet many individual stories remain unfamiliar to wider audiences. By using an interactive listening format, this project invites visitors to engage with one young person’s memories and to reflect on coercion, survival and the ways testimony is presented to the public.

The guiding question

What remained of youth under forced labour in Nazi Germany?

The project story

LISTENING TO THE PAST

In 1943, Mieczysław Gnojek was deported from Poland to Germany for forced labour at the age of fifteen. His recorded recollections are the starting point of this interactive audio story.

The rotary-telephone interface invites visitors to select parts of his story and listen to memories of life before deportation, forced labour and liberation. In bringing a historical voice into a contemporary digital setting, the project raises questions about how testimony should be interpreted and represented.

Developed by Natali Petala-Weber and Oleksandr Tsilyi, the project also emerged from a wider interest in the transnational history of forced labour, including Polish and Greek experiences.
