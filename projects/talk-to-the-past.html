The story

Talk to the Past is an interactive audio story created within the EVZ-funded project “Who Owns Memory?”. Through a rotary telephone, users connect to the memories of Mieczysław Gnojek, who was deported from Poland to Germany for forced labour in 1943 at the age of fifteen. The telephone frame is deliberately broken: no line ever led to the forced labourers, so each call connects not to the past but to memories written down decades later and read by people of today, in English, German and Polish. Recordings, transcripts, short explanations of key terms and reflection questions invite young people in particular to think about what it means to have one’s youth taken away, and about who decides what is remembered. All sources, the use of AI and the team’s editorial decisions are disclosed transparently on the page.

Where and when?

Originally: Youth experiences and memories in Forced Labour of Nazi-Germany across borders: Poland and Greece. The story is transnational and originally included archive material from a greek survivor.

Central public-history question

What was left of being young in forced labour in Nazi-Germany?

Why does this story matter?

Forced labour was the largest coerced population movement in modern European history and is still the most under-commemorated. Greek forced labour in particular is thinly researched compared with the Polish and Soviet cases.
