Talk to the Past

A rotary telephone, a young forced labourer, and the distance between memory and history

Talk to the Past is an interactive audio story created within the EVZ-funded project “Who Owns Memory?”

The public-history question
What was left of being young in forced labour in Nazi Germany?
Why this story matters

Forced labour was the largest coerced population movement in modern European history and is still the most under-commemorated. Greek forced labour in particular is thinly researched compared with the Polish and Soviet cases.

Deportation

A forced journey separated young people from family and familiar places. The project examines how that journey appears in later recorded memories.

Research basis: Gnojek’s published memoir, wartime records and the contributor’s submission.

CHAPTER 01

A telephone without a line to the past

The creators use the familiar rotary telephone as a way of approaching the memories of Mieczysław Gnojek, who was deported from Poland to Nazi Germany for forced labour in 1943, when he was fifteen. The visitor can imagine making a call, but no telephone line connects the present directly to the experience of a forced labourer.

CHAPTER 02

A youth shaped by coercion

The project draws on memoir material recorded and published long after the events. That distance matters: memories are documents in their own right, shaped by recollection, language, editorial decisions and the conditions under which people finally recount what happened.

CHAPTER 03

Who speaks for a remembered experience?

The telephone format invites questions about the ethics of narration. A person who listens in the present encounters a mediated account, not an unfiltered voice from 1943. The work asks what remained of youth under Nazi forced labour and how that experience should be publicly communicated.
