Shushi Beyond Reach

Memory, Loss and Belonging

Shushi Beyond Reach explores what happens to collective memory when physical access to a place is lost while the material environment that carries that memory is also transformed, endangered or destroyed

Lusine MkhitaryanDigital exhibition / web story

The public-history question
How do people remain connected to a place they can no longer physically return to?
Why this story matters

The story matters because the loss of physical access to a place does not erase the historical and cultural relationships built around it. In the case of Shushi, that rupture is compounded by the destruction, alteration and endangerment of material lafters through which Armenian historical continuity and collective memory have been carried. The destruction of cultural heritage also reaches beyond the community directly connected to it: each lost historical and cultural lafter diminishes the shared cultural record of humanity. By bringing together historical sources, documented heritage evidence and personal photographs, the project examines how connection to place can persist when both physical access and the material foundations of memory are disrupted.

Shushi / Shusha and memory

Memory, Loss and Belonging

Note on terminology. This project uses the Armenian name Shushi. In Azerbaijani, the city is

called Shusha. I use Shushi because this story focuses on Armenian collective memory, cultural

heritage, and my own relationship to the city; the terminology reflects that perspective.

CHAPTER 02

The material environment of memory

This story is not an attempt to present the entire history of Shushi. It seeks to understand what

happens to collective memory formed around a place when physical connection with that place is

broken, while the material environment that carries that memory is destroyed or altered and its

historical identity is distorted.
