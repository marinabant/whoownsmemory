Place & Local Memory

Shushi Beyond Reach: Memory, Loss and Belonging

Lusine Mkhitaryan•Digital exhibition / web story•Solo

The story

Shushi Beyond Reach explores what happens to collective memory when physical access to a place is lost while the material environment that carries that memory is also transformed, endangered or destroyed. Moving between 1920, 1992, 2020 and the present, the web story combines historical research, heritage monitoring, institutional sources and photographs taken in Shushi in 2016 to examine rupture, return, loss and the continuing relationship between memory and place.

Where and when?

The story is situated in Shushi, in Nagorno-Karabakh/Artsakh, and moves across four interconnected temporal layers: 1920, 1992, 2020 and the present. It focuses on Armenian collective memory and cultural heritage: the rupture of the Armenian urban and cultural environment in 1920; the way the events of 1992 came to be remembered in Armenian public memory through ideas of return, victory and rebirth; the loss of Armenian physical access to the city after the 2020 war; and the post-2023 context of displacement and digital memory preservation.

Central public-history question

How do people remain connected to a place they can no longer physically return to?

Why does this story matter?

The story matters because the loss of physical access to a place does not erase the historical and cultural relationships built around it. In the case of Shushi, that rupture is compounded by the destruction, alteration and endangerment of material layers through which Armenian historical continuity and collective memory have been carried. The destruction of cultural heritage also reaches beyond the community directly connected to it: each lost historical and cultural layer diminishes the shared cultural record of humanity. By bringing together historical sources, documented heritage evidence and personal photographs, the project examines how connection to place can persist when both physical access and the material foundations of memory are disrupted.

Sources & evidence

Grigoryan and G. Harutyunyan, The Shushi Real School, 2001; Gayane Makhmourian, “The Tragedy of the City of Shushi on March 23–26, 1920 in Armenian and Foreign Historiography,” Historical-Philological Journal, 2021; Geghard Scientific-Analytical Foundation, “The Massacres of the Armenians of Shushi in 1920,” 2024; Central Bank of Armenia, “15th Anniversary of Liberation of Shushi,” commemorative coin, 2007.

Public-history lens

This project is presented within Place, Local Memory, Monuments & Heritage. Read it not only for what happened in the past, but for how sources, memory, place, visibility and interpretation shape what becomes public history.
